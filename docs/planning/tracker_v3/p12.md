## Objective

**no judge at all**. Then use the hold-out once. The development part is a **bug check only** (the facts and
questions were designed on it; the user, 2026-10-23); **the result of p12 is the hold-out**. The weak-agent conversations keep the judge stand-in of p11: they

## Steps
1. **Approval request** in `docs/planning/tracker_v3/approval_request.md`:
   - the exact number of calls (from p8's dry-run script), for the strong-agent development data (about 1,400
     conversations: Stage A gpt-6-luna 1,000, plus our and the partner's train runs; the Stage B tries keep the judge stand-in) and the hold-out (198);
   - the model: gpt-6-luna, low effort;
   - the cost, from p8's counts × the measured price (estimate before the dry run: $0.3-13, point $3.5, for 2 to
     27 questions per conversation at $0.00008-0.0003 each), and a hard limit of 2 × the high estimate from the dry run
     (count × $0.0003), at least $10 (plan value $26), enforced by the budget stop of
     `questions.py` (p8);
   - the time;
   - the command.

   Then **wait** for the user's approval.
2. **Run** the questions from the main chat. Answers are saved by (question, message).
3. **Bug check on a development sample** (`meaning = tiny`; the user, 2026-10-23: a sample saves time): the first 50
   conversations of each of the 4 strong-agent development data sets (200 conversations, `--limit-convs 50`). P12.1's zero-count marks, and the answer problems
   counted (unclear answers, failed quotes, two keys that disagree). Not p11's counts, labels, length split or audit:
   those are in-sample and are not results. A failure is fixed here, on development data, and logged. When step 4
   starts, v3 is frozen for p14.

   **What happened (2026-10-23):** P12.1 passed, but the tiny guard was far stricter than the judge (207 vs 31 blocks
   on the 200 conversations), mainly K2. Fixed with the user's approval (`k2_fix.md`): K2 version 2, check lines built
   from the call's arguments; the quote check ignores `**` and line breaks; a read-only review of K13, K1, K5 (changes
   only with the user's approval). The changed questions are re-asked on the same 200 conversations (about $0.05,
   limit $1, inside p12's approval), and this step runs again before step 4.
4. **Hold-out, once (the offline result):**
   - τ-bench tool labels (p3) for the val conversations and the Stage B tries forked from them;
   - the tiny questions on the hold-out (in the same approval; `v3_p8_dryrun.py`'s real mode needs a small $0
     change to allow the hold-out, with its own budget stop);
   - then p11's test on them, with the tiny answers: the counts, the classes of changed decisions, the audit buckets
     and the length split;
   - the tiny answers vs the judge's saved answers: every disagreement on a blocked check is listed and reviewed by an
     independent agent against the policy text, as a second opinion. Also counted: unclear answers, failed quotes,
     two-key disagreements.

   The zero-count marks must hold there (P12.2). Nothing is fixed on the hold-out.
5. **Independent recount on the hold-out:** a fresh agent, which has not seen p11's code, recomputes the hold-out's
   main counts from the plan text. Pass: equal counts (P12.3).
6. **Report** `output/eval_v3/p12_report.md`: the hold-out first (the result), then the development bug check, marked
   "development check, not a result". Figures from the hold-out (the share decided by an LLM: frozen vs v3; tiny vs
   judge disagreements by question), and a recommendation on p13-p14. Then **STOP**.
